# Knowledge card: Executive Order 12, Chief Savings Officers

**In one line:** signed January 29, 2026, it puts a Chief Savings Officer in every agency to find recurring structural savings, reporting to the First Deputy Mayor and OMB.

## What the order requires

- Every agency head names a Chief Savings Officer. The officer reports to the agency head, sits with budget and finance leadership, and has access to staff and data.
- Those officers engage regularly with the Office of the First Deputy Mayor and the Mayor's Office of Management and Budget.
- Savings have to reduce the agency's baseline, not land as a one-time fix. Section 2 names four levers: more efficient delivery, consolidating programs, insourcing work done by vendors, and eliminating or sunsetting programs.
- Within 45 days, the agency looks at its five most costly expense-funded programs, the initiatives with the strongest results, and programs that could run more efficiently, including programs created in the past year.
- Updates follow every six months.

## What has been published

- January 29, 2026: the order is signed. The stated targets were 1.5% in FY2026 and 2.5% in FY2027.
- March 2026: agencies identify more than $1.7 billion. The Executive Budget later states $1.77 billion over FY2026 and FY2027.
- July 2026: another 2.5% for FY2027 and FY2028, starting from a workforce survey, aimed at the November Financial Plan. City Hall said layoffs were not the tool.

The published $1.77 billion was not all operational change. The Executive Budget summary split it into financial management and re-estimates ($947 million), more efficient services including overtime and unused programs ($368 million), vacancies ($307 million), contracts ($94 million), technology ($28 million), and space ($24 million). The operational slice is the part this pane is built to inform. This repo does not restate those dollars as findings of its own.

## How the pane speaks to the order

- A goal whose plan moves sharply while most indicators move against their aim is a public version of "is this program working?"
- Overtime that rises faster than expenditures is a public version of the cost-driver review. It is the same lever as more efficient delivery, shown inside the agency zoom, not as a separate product.
- Each flag is labeled with the lever it resembles. The label is a hypothesis. The pane does not claim a dollar saved.
